## Brief (from the kit's RULES.txt, judge brief in full)

Write one sentence, up to 200 characters, about the best advice the writer ever
received. A judge model compares entries pairwise and the line that carries the
most lived truth wins. The judge weighs, most important first:

1. It is a single sentence about the best advice the writer ever received.
2. The advice is worth carrying: specific, earned, and true beyond the moment
   it came from.
3. It sounds like a real voice recalling a real moment, not a slogan or a
   fortune cookie.
4. It is tight: every word earns its place and the whole thing reads in one breath.
5. It lands, leaving the reader with something to keep after the last word.

"A wise-sounding line with no person behind it reads as filler. Cleverness for
its own sake does not count."

## Authoring prompt used in this session

Write six candidate one-liners in English, each a single sentence of at most 200
characters, each framed as advice actually given by a named person in a named
moment. Prefer concrete scenes (a wedding eve, a failed test, a first job) over
generic wisdom. The advice must carry a twist that stays true beyond the moment.
Rank them against the five judge criteria above, pick the strongest, then revise
it once with a real change to the text (not cosmetics) before finalizing.
